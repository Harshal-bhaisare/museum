      About the Project

      The Road to Independence

      Indonesia's independence was not a single event but the outcome of a long historical process. It began with the rise of organized nationalism and youth movements in the early twentieth century, groups that gradually encouraged people of different ethnic and regional backgrounds to imagine themselves as one Indonesian nation.

      The Japanese occupation during the Second World War disrupted Dutch colonial rule and created new, if difficult, opportunities for Indonesian leaders to organize politically. The Proclamation of Independence on 17 August 1945 transformed that movement into the foundation of a republic — one that then had to be defended through revolution, armed struggle and diplomacy.

      The transfer of sovereignty in 1949 marked the formal end of Dutch colonial rule, but it was not the end of the story. Independence continued to shape Indonesia's political identity, institutions and nation-building in the decades that followed.
